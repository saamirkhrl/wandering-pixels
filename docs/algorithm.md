# Matched-photometry readout: has this star changed between visits?

Specification for the website's "Has it changed between visits?" answer. Reference implementation:
`validation/wp/m2ref.py` (Python, numpy, astropy, photutils). Test vectors:
`docs/algorithm_test_vectors.json`, regenerated by `cd validation && uv run python scripts/09_algorithm_vectors.py`.
Validation behind the choices: `docs/method.md` and `docs/results.json`.

## Summary

The user clicks a star in the SPHEREx images and asks whether its brightness differs between two
visits (about six months or a year apart). SPHEREx observes each star at a different wavelength in
every exposure, so two exposures of the same constant star differ simply because they sit at
different wavelengths, and a plain "this exposure minus that exposure" misreads more constant stars
as changed the larger the wavelength gap is. The readout therefore fits, for each visit separately,
a straight line through that star's own measurements at nearby wavelengths, evaluates both visits at
the same wavelength, and compares those values. Several independent wavelengths are combined into
one number and one significance, and the plain pair and a simpler paired average are shown beside it.

## Inputs

All inputs are for ONE detector d. The browser loads one detector's exposures (about 20 per visit),
so every exposure below belongs to the same detector. Names in brackets are the data library's
(`src/lib/spherex`, roundtable files 05, 07, 08, 09).

| Input | Meaning |
|---|---|
| P | `IMAGE - ZODI`, MJy/sr, native pixels of the exposure (library: `Frame.pixels`, see "Conflicts" for the resampling caveat) |
| VAR | `VARIANCE` plane, (MJy/sr)^2 (`Frame.variance`) |
| FLAGS | raw `FLAGS` plane, 32-bit (`Frame.flags` only carries a collapsed 8-bit form; see "Conflicts") |
| WCS | spatial WCS of the exposure (pixel coordinates 0-based, `data[y, x]`, pixel (i, j) covers [i-0.5, i+0.5]) |
| mjd | `MJD-AVG` (`Exposure.mjd`) |
| visit | visit index (`Exposure.visitIndex`). Only exposures with `inStack = true`, i.e. in visits of at least 12 exposures (`Exposure.inStack`), are used |
| lam, bw | wavelength and bandwidth (um) at the star's detector pixel, per exposure |
| star | Gaia DR3 position at epoch 2016.0, proper motion, optional parallax, G |
| Gaia cone | optional, for the `blended` flag: sources within 20 arcsec with their G |

Star position at each exposure: Gaia position propagated to `mjd` with proper motion
(`positionAt` in the library). The reference script also adds parallax (`wp.gaia.propagate`); for
parallax below 100 mas the difference is under 0.02 pixel and does not matter here.

Wavelength: lam and bw come either from the spectral WCS calibration (CWAVE and CBAND, nearest
pixel) or from the `WCS-WAVE` lookup table in each file (bilinear). The two differ by a median of
about 0.3 nm and at most about 7 nm, which is below 0.2 of a bandwidth (`wavelength_source_check` in
`docs/results.json`: medians 0.17 to 0.59 nm over detectors D1 to D6, target maxima 7.3 nm at Eps
Indi and 2.6 nm at Barnard's Star, 0.19 and 0.046 of the bandwidth). Either is acceptable. The test
vectors use the calibration (CWAVE, CBAND). The wavelength must be read at the star's own pixel in
each exposure (the library's `wavelengthMap` and `bandwidthMap` do this), not at the field centre.

Units: F below is MJy/sr summed over native pixels. To get flux density multiply by the pixel solid
angle: (6.15 / 206265)^2 = 8.890e-10 sr, so 1 MJy/sr summed over one native pixel is 888.99 microJy
(0.889 mJy). The archive publishes a solid-angle pixel map; using it per pixel is a refinement, not
needed for a ratio or a significance.

## Steps

### 1. Pixel mask

A pixel is excluded when `FLAGS & BAD_DATA_MASK != 0` (mask = sum of 2^bit for the BAD_DATA bits
above), or P or VAR is non-finite, or VAR <= 0 (the last test is an addition to the library mask, as in
`validation/wp/forced.py`). Bits 1 (OVERFLOW), 17, 20, 25, 28 and 29 are not masked.

### 2. Per-exposure photometry

For one star in one exposure, with (x, y) the star in 0-based native pixels:

1. Reject if the star is outside the cutout, or the aperture is not fully inside it
   (x - r < -0.5, x + r > nx - 0.5, same for y).
2. Aperture weights w_k: exact overlap area of the circle (radius 2.0, centre (x, y)) with each pixel
   (photutils `CircularAperture(...).to_mask(method="exact")`). Pixels with w_k > 0 form the aperture.
   Reject the exposure for this star if any aperture pixel is excluded by step 1.
3. Background: from the pixels not excluded by step 1 and with 4.0 <= r < 7.0 (r = distance of the
   pixel centre to (x, y)), do a 3-sigma, 5-iteration sigma clip around the median. b = median of the
   kept pixels, s_b = their standard deviation, N_b = their count. Reject if the annulus has fewer
   than 10 pixels before or after clipping.
4. F = sum_k w_k (P_k - b).
5. var_F = sum_k w_k^2 VAR_k + (sum_k w_k)^2 (pi / 2) s_b^2 / N_b.
6. Inflate: var' = var_F + (0.02 F)^2. From here on only var' is used.
7. Quality flag `saturated`: the raw FLAGS in the central 5 x 5 pixels (centre = nearest pixel to the
   star) carry bit 1, 15 or 26. It is set whenever the star is inside the cutout, also for an exposure
   that is then rejected. It does not reject anything by itself; bits 15 and 26 are in BAD_DATA and
   therefore also reject the exposure if they fall inside the aperture.

An accepted exposure is the record (id, mjd, lam, bw, F, var', saturated).

### 3. Per-visit spectral model

Given one visit's accepted exposures (detector d), an evaluation wavelength w0 and the local
bandwidth b0 (the bandwidth of the exposure that defines w0):

1. Samples: exposures with |lam - w0| <= 2.5 b0 (and finite F, var' > 0).
2. Require at least 3 samples, at least one with lam <= w0 and one with lam >= w0 (no extrapolation).
   Otherwise the result is "no value": reason `too_few_samples` or `no_bracket`.
3. Weighted least squares of F = a + c (lam - w0), weights 1 / var'. With x_i = lam_i - w0 and
   S = sum 1/var'_i, S_x = sum x_i/var'_i, S_xx = sum x_i^2/var'_i, S_y = sum F_i/var'_i,
   S_xy = sum x_i F_i/var'_i, Dn = S S_xx - S_x^2:
   a = (S_xx S_y - S_x S_xy) / Dn, c = (S S_xy - S_x S_y) / Dn, Var(a) = S_xx / Dn.
4. Normalised residuals |F_i - (a + c x_i)| / sqrt(var'_i). If the largest exceeds 4, drop that one
   sample and refit once (only one sample is ever dropped; a full clipping pass from a fit pulled by one
   outlier can reject every sample).
5. Require the bracket and at least 3 samples again after the drop.
6. Matched flux = a (the model value at w0), matched variance = Var(a).

This is the same computation as `validation/wp/phaseb.spec_model`; `m2ref.assert_spec_equals_phaseb()`
checks equality on random windows (300 trials: 200 values compared, 100 failures compared, 71 with a
clipped sample; all equal to 1e-9 relative).

### 4. Common wavelengths of a visit pair

Visit A is the earlier visit, B the later. Candidates are visit A's accepted exposures sorted by
(lam, id). Select greedily from the shortest wavelength: take the first, then each next candidate
whose lam is more than 5 b0 above the last selected lam (b0 = that candidate's bw). With the 2.5 b0
half width this makes the windows disjoint and the per-wavelength comparisons independent. Selection
does not depend on whether the models succeed. For each selected w0 (with b0):

- F_A(w0), var_A: step 3 on visit A; F_B(w0), var_B: step 3 on visit B with the same w0 and b0.
- D(w0) = F_A - F_B, var = var_A + var_B, z = D / sqrt(var).
- A w0 where either model has no value is skipped and recorded with its reason.

### 5. Summary of a pair

Over the selected w0 that were not skipped:

- D_comb = sum(D / var) / sum(1 / var), var_comb = 1 / sum(1 / var), z_comb = D_comb / sqrt(var_comb).
- Fractional change = D_comb / Fbar, where Fbar = sum(0.5 (F_A + F_B) / var) / sum(1 / var) (same
  weights as D).
- Verdict text: |z_comb| < 3 gives "no significant change", |z_comb| >= 3 gives "changed". The
  threshold 3 was calibrated on steady stars (results.json thresholds_barnard and
  thresholds_epsindi; one value per star, detector and visit pair): for this matched readout,
  |z| >= 3 occurs for 0.87 percent of Barnard comparisons (68 percent interval 0.62 to 1.22,
  N = 1258) and 0.81 percent of Eps Indi (N = 1112); |z| >= 5 for 0.16 and 0.00 percent. Use
  |z| >= 5 for about 0.1 percent false alarms. The plain pair and the paired stack have heavier
  tails (|z| >= 3: 1.3 to 3.6 percent) and need about 4 for 1 percent. The word "changed" is a statement about the measured fluxes, not
  a classification of the cause.
- If no w0 survives the result is "no result" (flags explain why).

### 6. Comparison outputs for the UI

Plain best pair. For each selected, non-skipped w0, take the visit-A exposure that defines it and the
visit-B accepted exposure whose lam at the star is nearest to it (ties: smaller id). D_plain =
F(A exposure) - F(B exposure) with the same inflated variances, z_plain = D_plain / sqrt(var'_A + var'_B),
and also the inverse-variance mean over the selected w0 (as in step 5) with its z and fractional
change. This is what a plain two-exposure difference gives.

Paired stack PS (validated as nearly equal to the matched result). Form all (visit-A exposure,
visit-B exposure) pairs whose |lam_A - lam_B| <= 1.0 x 0.5 (bw_A + bw_B). Sort by |lam gap|, then by
|mjd_A - mjd_B|, then by obs_id. Accept pairs greedily so each exposure is used at most once. Take the
inverse-variance mean of the pair differences (weights 1 / (var'_A + var'_B)), with variance
1 / sum(weights), z, and fractional change as in step 5 using 0.5 (F_A + F_B).

### 7. Outputs and flags

Per (star, visit pair):

| Output | Content |
|---|---|
| per visit, per w0 | matched flux F and error sqrt(var), number of samples used |
| per w0 | D, z (and D_plain, z_plain) |
| combined | D_comb, z_comb, fractional change, verdict |
| plain | D_plain, z_plain (combined, and per w0) |
| PS | D, z, fractional change, number of pairs |
| counts | accepted exposures per visit, number of w0 used, number of w0 skipped with reasons |
| flags | see below |

Flags:

- `saturated`: any measured exposure of either visit carries the saturation quality flag of step 2.
- `too_few_samples`: at least one selected w0 was skipped for too few samples.
- `no_bracket`: at least one selected w0 was skipped because it would need extrapolation (usually the
  short or long end of the detector's band).
- `blended`: a Gaia neighbour with G < G_star + 3 within 20 arcsec (needs a Gaia cone; omit if not
  available). `m2ref.is_blended` is the reference.
- `partial_visit`: fewer than 8 accepted exposures in either visit.

## Details the steps leave implicit (fixed by the reference implementation)

A port must copy these; `validation/wp/m2ref.py` is the authority.

Exposure rejection (step 2), checked in this order; the first that applies is the reason:

| reason | applies when |
|---|---|
| `outside_cutout` | the star centre is outside [-0.5, nx - 0.5] x [-0.5, ny - 0.5] (or non-finite) |
| `edge` | the aperture is not fully inside the cutout: x - r < -0.5, x + r > nx - 0.5, same for y, or the aperture mask's pixel box leaves the array |
| `masked_aperture` | any pixel with weight w_k > 0 is excluded by step 1 |
| `annulus_few` | fewer than 10 unmasked finite pixels in the annulus, or fewer than 10 kept after the clip |
| `ok` | accepted |

The `saturated` quality flag is computed as soon as the star centre is inside the cutout, before the
other checks (the 5 x 5 window is cut at the array edge, centre = nearest pixel with ties to even),
so a rejected exposure can still carry it. Exposures whose lam or bw is not finite are not accepted
(not tested by the reference; the library supplies finite values).

Spectral model skips (step 3 and 4):
- The two tests are `too_few_samples` (fewer than 3 samples) then `no_bracket` (no sample with
  lam <= w0 or none with lam >= w0, both inclusive). They are applied twice: on the window samples,
  and again after the single outlier drop. `too_few_samples` is tested first each time.
- Window membership is |lam - w0| <= 2.5 b0 (inclusive) with finite F and var' > 0.
- The outlier test uses each sample's own var' in the normalised residual; the single worst sample
  (first one on a tie) is dropped only if its residual is strictly greater than 4.
- A w0 is skipped when the model of visit A or of visit B has no value. The recorded reason is visit A's
  reason if A failed, otherwise B's. The visit-A exposure that defines w0 is itself in the window with
  x = 0, so visit A always brackets itself; a `no_bracket` skip therefore means visit B lacks samples on
  one side, and `too_few_samples` can come from either visit.
- A skipped w0 is excluded from D_comb, var_comb, the fractional-change denominator, the plain-pair
  means and `n_w0`, but it still counts for selection (it consumed its place in the 5 b0 spacing).
  Flag `too_few_samples` / `no_bracket` is set if at least one skipped w0 has that recorded reason.

w0 selection (step 4):
- Candidates are visit A's accepted exposures, sorted ascending by (lam, obs_id as a string).
- The first candidate is always selected. A later candidate is selected if
  lam > lam_last_selected + 5 x bw_candidate (strictly greater; the bandwidth is the candidate's own,
  not the last selected one's). The comparison is made against the last selected, not the last
  considered, candidate.
- b0 for the model window is the selected exposure's own bw, used for both visits.

Weights and fractional change (steps 5 and 6):
- All inverse-variance weights use the inflated variances (var' of step 2). For matched values the
  weight of each w0 is 1 / var with var = var_A + var_B (the model variances from step 3, which in turn
  come from var').
- Fractional change = D_comb / Fbar with Fbar = sum(m_i / var_i) / sum(1 / var_i), m_i = 0.5 (F_A + F_B)
  at w0_i (the matched fluxes), and var_i the same weights as for D_comb.
- Plain pair per w0: var_plain = var'(A exposure) + var'(B exposure); the combined plain D is the
  inverse-variance mean of D_plain with weights 1 / var_plain; the plain fractional change divides it
  by sum(m_i / var_plain_i) / sum(1 / var_plain_i) with the same matched m_i. The plain list uses the
  same non-skipped w0 as the matched result, and the same visit-B exposure may be chosen for two w0.
- Plain partner choice: the accepted visit-B exposure minimising |lam_B - w0|; ties go to the smaller
  obs_id (string comparison).
- Paired stack: candidate pairs satisfy |lam_A - lam_B| <= 1.0 x 0.5 (bw_A + bw_B) (inclusive). They are
  sorted lexicographically by (|lam gap|, |mjd_A - mjd_B|, obs_id_A, obs_id_B) and accepted in that
  order if neither exposure is already used. The PS fractional change divides the pair-weighted mean
  difference by the pair-weighted mean of 0.5 (F_A + F_B) of the measured (not modelled) fluxes, with
  weights 1 / (var'_A + var'_B). It uses all accepted exposures, not only the selected w0.
- z = D / sqrt(var) everywhere; the verdict uses z_comb only (|z_comb| >= 3 is "changed"). PS has no verdict.

Flags (step 7):
- `saturated` is set if any measured exposure of either visit (accepted or rejected) carries the step 2
  quality flag. `partial_visit` is set if either visit has fewer than 8 accepted exposures (not the
  number in the window). `blended` uses small-angle separations, any other Gaia source (the star itself
  excluded) within 20 arcsec (inclusive) with G strictly below G_star + 3. When flags are listed they are
  ordered saturated, partial_visit, blended, too_few_samples, no_bracket (an ordering convention only).
- With no surviving w0 the combined fields are empty and the verdict is "no result"; PS can still be
  present.
- Positions: x, y of the star are evaluated per exposure from the WCS at that exposure's epoch; no
  shift or recentring is done. The background is a median (not a mean) with s_b the standard deviation
  (ddof 0) of the kept pixels; astropy `sigma_clip` with `cenfunc="median"`, `stdfunc="std"`, sigma 3,
  at most 5 iterations.

## Failure cases

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| star outside the cutout, aperture on the edge, masked aperture pixel, short annulus | exposure not used for this star |
| fewer than 3 samples in a window, or w0 not bracketed | that w0 skipped, flagged |
| no w0 survives | "no result", flags explain |
| visit with fewer than 12 exposures | not a visit, not used (library `inStack`) |
| saturated or bloomed star | numbers produced, `saturated` flag shown; not validated (see limits) |
| neighbour within 20 arcsec | `blended` flag; the 12.3 arcsec aperture includes part of it |
| only one visit available | no comparison possible |

## What was validated

All numbers are from `docs/results.json`; each is quoted with the bootstrap error given there
(bootstraps resample stars). Samples: 70 Barnard's Star field stars and 49 Eps Indi field stars (Gaia
G 12 to 16.5, non-variable, isolated), all six detectors; "M2" is this method on native pixels, "one"
means the model uses only the same detector as here.

- Gap dependence (`phaseb_controls_barnard`, `phaseb_controls_epsindi`). The robust width of z for the
  plain pair grows with the wavelength gap, by 0.636 +- 0.090 sigma per channel in Barnard's field and
  0.175 +- 0.022 sigma per channel in the Eps Indi field. For the matched method the slope is
  0.0011 +- 0.0011 and 0.0001 +- 0.0010 sigma per channel, consistent with flat. The fractional scatter
  of the plain pair grows by 2.94 +- 0.11 and 2.88 +- 0.07 percentage points per channel, the matched
  one by 0.0013 +- 0.0024 and -0.0027 +- 0.0084.
- Matched against plain pair at the same star and visit pair (`diff_M2_minus_P1native_scatter`): the
  matched fractional scatter is lower by 2.96 +- 0.15 percentage points (Barnard) and 5.90 +- 0.45
  (Eps Indi).
- Colour dependence (`phaseb_metrics_barnard`, `phaseb_metrics_epsindi`): slope of the fractional
  difference against BP-RP of 0.005 +- 0.267 and -0.307 +- 0.367 percent per magnitude, i.e. no
  colour trend detected.
- Straight line against constant (`diff_M2_minus_M2flat_scatter`): -0.84 +- 0.07 percentage points
  (Barnard, significant) and -0.16 +- 0.15 (Eps Indi, not significant).
- M2 against the paired stack PS (`ps_vs_m2_tol1_one_diff_scatter`; PS minus M2): +0.43 +- 0.08
  percentage points in Barnard's field with one detector (M2 better) and +0.28 +- 0.20 in the Eps
  Indi field (not significant). With all six detectors in the model the difference is +0.13 +- 0.07
  and +0.15 +- 0.13, not significant. So M2 and PS agree within the errors in most cases, and M2 is
  better with one detector in Barnard's field by 0.4 to 0.5 percentage points of fractional scatter
  (0.43 +- 0.08 at the 1-channel tolerance, 0.53 +- 0.08 at 0.5, 0.46 +- 0.06 at 2).
- Pipeline variances are too small. The leave-one-out residuals of the per-visit model are wider than
  their propagated errors, and a fractional term added in quadrature (var' = var + (b F)^2) restores a
  width of 1. Global fits of b are in `err_b_add_global` of both control sections (no bootstrap error
  was computed for them); they differ between the two fields, so the constant 0.02 used here is
  an empirical round value, not a fitted one. The z widths in the control tables above were computed
  before this term was added.

## Limits

- The 2 percent error floor is empirical (see above). The significance `z` is therefore only as good
  as that floor, and the 3-sigma display threshold is not a calibrated false-positive rate.
- Saturated and bloomed stars are not handled. Their cores are masked (bits 15, 26) or carry bit 1,
  the aperture then rejects or the flux is biased; the flag is shown but the numbers are not
  validated (test star 4 below is one: G 12.47, the saturation flag is set in 14 of 18 exposures per
  visit).
- Crowded stars: the 12.3 arcsec aperture and 4 to 7 pixel annulus include neighbours; only the
  `blended` flag warns.
- One detector covers only its own band (about 0.75 to 5 um in six bands); a star is compared only
  at wavelengths bracketed by that detector's samples in both visits, so band edges give no value.
- The combined number is an inverse-variance mean over only a few independent wavelengths per
  detector (two or three in the test vectors). Differences of opposite sign at different wavelengths
  can cancel in D_comb (test star 4: z of -2.30 and +3.77 at its two wavelengths, combined -0.10). Show
  the per-wavelength values.
- Validated on stars of G 12 to 16.5 in two fields, with visit pairs of about 6, 12 and 18 months. Not
  validated for variable stars, faint stars, or a pair of visits less than a month apart.

## Conflicts with the data library as it stands

Found while reading roundtable files 05, 07, 08, 09 and `src/lib/spherex` (read only):

1. `Frame.pixels` and `Frame.variance` are bilinearly resampled onto the common grid (`frame.ts`),
   not native pixels. The method was validated on native pixels with exact overlap weights. On the
   grid the pixel errors are correlated (the variance is propagated as if independent) and a bilinear
   kernel smooths the PSF. A port should read native-pixel windows (the library already reads Range
   windows of the Level 2 file) or accept an unvalidated variant.
2. `Frame.flags` is a collapsed `Uint8`. Raw bit 1 (OVERFLOW) is not carried, so the `saturated` flag
   of step 2 can only use bits 15 and 26 (library `SATURATED`), not bit 1. Bits 15 and 26 are already in
   `BAD_DATA_MASK`.
3. `spectrumAt` uses a binary in-aperture test, a plain (unclipped) median annulus from 4 to 8 pixels
   (`rIn = max(r + 2, 2 r)`, `rOut = rIn + 4`) and the variance of the median 1.2533^2 s^2 / N, and its
   `fluxErr` has no floor. Steps 2 differs in each respect (exact overlap, 4 to 7 pixels, 3-sigma
   clipped median, 2 percent floor).
4. `Exposure.wavelengthAtTarget` is evaluated at the target position, not at a given star. The method
   needs lam and bw at each star in each exposure; use `wavelengthMap` and `bandwidthMap`
   (`FrameOptions.wavelengthMap`).
5. Visit rule: the library splits visits longer than 90 days into 60-day bins and marks clusters of
   fewer than 12 exposures `inStack = false`; the validation used the equivalent rule "at least 12
   exposures". Compatible in practice (Barnard: visits 0, 2, 4 are the full visits).
6. `Frame.pixels` is already NaN for BAD_DATA pixels, so step 1 reduces to a NaN test on the library's
   side; VAR <= 0 is not tested there.

## Test vectors

`docs/algorithm_test_vectors.json` holds four real stars of the Barnard's Star field, detector 2, visit 0
(earlier) against visit 2 (about 6 months later). Nothing in the file is rounded: every number is a
float64 written with its round-trip representation, and the expected outputs are exactly what
`m2ref.compare_visits` returns for the inputs in the file, so a port should match to 1e-6 relative.
`scripts/09_algorithm_vectors.py` ends with a self-check that reloads the JSON and recomputes every
expected output (per w0, combined, plain, paired stack, flags, and the stamp photometry) from the JSON
inputs alone.

The stars were chosen by rules fixed before any output was inspected. Eligible: the 70 phaseb sample
stars with at least 8 browser-mask measurements on detector 2 in each of the two visits (all 70
qualified). Star 4 (the saturated one): G closest to 12.5. Stars 2 and 3: G closest to 14.0 and 15.5.
Star 1: the star with G closest to 12.5 that has no saturation flag in any detector-2 exposure of the
two visits and at least 12 accepted exposures per visit (star 4 fails this).

For each star the file gives the Gaia input (position at 2016.0, proper motion, parallax, G), for each
exposure the star pixel position (x, y) in the cutout, lam, bw, whether it was accepted (reason if not),
`saturated`, F, raw and inflated variance, background and annulus count, a 17 x 17 pixel stamp (P, VAR,
raw FLAGS; P and VAR carried to 9 significant figures, and the expected photometry is computed from
those) of one accepted exposure (a pixel-level test of steps 1 and 2), and the expected outputs of
steps 3 to 7. The matched comparison takes as input exactly the exposures with `accepted = true`
(id = obs_id, lam, bw, mjd, F, var, saturated); the `saturated` output flag is set when any listed
exposure has `saturated = true`. F is in MJy/sr summed over native pixels. The values below are rounded
to 4 significant figures for reading only.
